How to play

            
          

          
Fill every row, column, and 3×3 box with the digits 1–9, without repeats.

          
Controls

          
    
            
  • Select a cell, then choose a number or type 1–9.
    • 
            
  • Turn on Notes to add or remove pencil marks.
    • 
            
  • Conflicting entries are highlighted immediately. Undo and redo are always available.
    • 
          

          
Hints that teach

          

            Hint starts with deductions you can prove. If you reach a dead end, it explains
            where to backtrack instead of silently replacing your work. Apply performs that
            one move but keeps you in control of your own game.
          

          
A repeatable solving loop

          
    
            
  1. Choose an empty cell and list the digits 1–9 that might fit.
    2. 
            
  2. Cross out every digit already used in the same row, column, or 3×3 box.
    3. 
            
  3. If one candidate remains, place it: that is a naked single.
    4. 
            
  4. Scan each row, column, and box. If a digit has only one possible home, place that hidden single.
    5. 
            
  5. Repeat the scans after every placement, because one move can unlock another.
    6. 
          

          

            If logic stalls, choose the empty cell with the fewest candidates, try one as a
            reversible assumption, and keep applying the loop. A contradiction means undo
            that branch and try its next candidate. The guess is controlled, never random.
          

          
Solver replay

          

            Replay is different from Hint: it takes over the board to review a complete solver
            path. Open the Puzzle panel and use Next or Previous to study one move at a time.
            Run plays at the selected speed, Pause keeps your place, and Finish jumps to the
            completed board. Blue digits are deductions; orange digits are reversible guesses.
            The explanation below the controls names the technique and its reason.
